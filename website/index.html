The everyday problem

Knowing an app exists is different from knowing what to do next.

Phone tasks rarely arrive as neat instructions. You have to recognise an icon, read a name, decide whether a prompt is safe, and remember what happens after the next tap.

Nook breaks that journey into small, repeatable steps, so a parent can learn the action—not just hear an explanation once.

Safety in context

The important pause happens inside the lesson.

Practising a payment includes checking who receives the money. Nook also shows pretend requests so a parent can practise saying no to a PIN request for receiving money.

In practice mode, the screens and money are pretend. Nook never asks for a real payment or real PIN in a lesson.

New in Stay safe · about 3 minutes

A screen interrupts you. What would you tap?

Some prompts are ordinary. Others push you to act quickly. Nook lets you make the choice on three pretend screens, then explains the result—without changing anything on your phone.

01 · A genuine permission

Maps asks for your location

Choose access that fits the task, or say no and enter a place yourself. A permission request is a choice, not automatically a scam.

02 · A pushy ad

A “cleaner update” counts down

You did not ask to install a cleaner. Try closing the ad or cancelling it; a timer does not verify that an update is needed.

03 · An unexpected message

A parcel text asks for your address

Practise leaving the unverified link and checking delivery independently through the service you trust.

Nothing real happens in this activity. No app installs, message sends, or phone setting changes. It is available in English, Hindi and Malayalam.
